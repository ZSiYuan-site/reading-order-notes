# Medicare 2027: what changed from 2026

Every November CMS publishes next year's Medicare premiums and deductibles. This year's release came out on November 17, 2026. Here are the changes that matter, side by side.

| Item | 2026 | 2027 |
|---|---|---|
| Part B standard premium (monthly) | $202.90 | $209.40 |
| Part B deductible | $283 | $292 |
| Part A hospital deductible | $1,736 | $1,776 |
| Part A premium if you must buy it (30 to 39 quarters) | $311 | $328 |
| Part A premium if you must buy it (under 30 quarters) | $565 | $597 |

## What the Part B increase means in dollars

The $6.50 monthly increase adds up to $78 over a year, so a person who pays the standard amount will pay $2,512.80 for Part B in 2027, compared with $2,434.80 in 2026. CMS says the increase is mainly due to projected growth in healthcare spending.

## Who pays more than the standard amount

People whose income was above $112,000 (single) or $224,000 (married filing jointly) on their 2025 tax return pay an income-related monthly adjustment amount, usually called IRMAA. In 2027 the surcharge steps from $83.70 up to $502.40 a month, and a separate Part D surcharge steps from $15.40 up to $96.40 a month.

## Why bracket edges matter

Because the brackets are cliffs, not slopes, one extra dollar of income can add $83.70 a month. That is $1,004.40 a year. Anyone close to $112,000 or $224,000 should check which side of the line they are on before a large IRA withdrawal or Roth conversion, since those count as income. (This is general information, not tax advice.)
