# CPSC Product Recalls by Hazard: A Practical Reading Guide

Most recall coverage is organised by brand or by date. That is fine if you already know the name on the box, but it is the wrong shape for the question people actually ask: what kinds of things are being recalled, and which of them could be in my home right now? Reading the notices by hazard is a more useful way in, because the same failure mode tends to repeat across products and manufacturers. This guide walks through the main hazard groups and explains what to look for in each one.

## Fire and burn

The [fire and burn hazard index](https://recalls.resuhive.com/hazard/fire-burn/) is the biggest and the easiest to understand. It covers rugs that fail a flammability standard, portable generators that can ignite, fire pits that burn unexpectedly and air fryers that overheat. The Boshengya shag rug notice, dated November 12, 2026, is a typical example: the rugs violate the mandatory standard for carpet and rug flammability, and the remedy is a refund after the owner destroys the rug. Cummins portable generators and Gourmia nine-slice air fryer ovens sit in the same broad group, though their remedies differ.

## Choking and ingestion

Small parts are the recurring theme on the [choking and ingestion page](https://recalls.resuhive.com/hazard/choking-ingestion/). Baby rattle and teether sets, magnetic building cubes and remote-controlled car toys with button batteries all appear here. The SHEIN baby rattle notice and the [magnet building cube recall](https://recalls.resuhive.com/recall/magnetic-building-cubes-and-blocks-27076/) are worth reading side by side: one violates the small parts ban, the other the magnet ingestion rule. The practical check is always the same. If a part fits through a toilet paper tube, keep it away from young children.

## Suffocation and strangulation

Nursing pillows and children's sensory swings show up in the [suffocation and strangulation group](https://recalls.resuhive.com/hazard/suffocation-strangulation/). These recalls often involve a soft surface or a strap rather than a sharp edge, and the age guidance on the packaging is usually the detail that matters most.

## Falls and tip-overs

The [fall and tip-over index](https://recalls.resuhive.com/hazard/fall-tip-over/) gathers dressers, ladders, trikes and ice axes. Tall furniture that can tip over is a surprisingly common entry, and the fix is often an anchoring strap rather than a return.

## Shock and impact

Finally, the [electric shock hazard](https://recalls.resuhive.com/hazard/electric-shock/) and the laceration and impact group cover pressure washers, lithium-ion battery packs and lighting fixtures. A pressure washer recall may involve an electrocution risk, while a battery pack recall usually means a fire or overheating risk instead.

Recall Radar keeps 630 U.S. recalls from the past year in one searchable index, updated daily from the CPSC. If you are short on time, open the [this week view](https://recalls.resuhive.com/this-week/) first, then drill into the hazard that matches the product in your hand. Reading by hazard turns a long list of notices into a short checklist you can actually use.
