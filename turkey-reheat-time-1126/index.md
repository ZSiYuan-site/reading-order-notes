# Reheat turkey by method

A reheat is a small cooking job with a surprisingly large number of ways to get it wrong. Too much heat drives off moisture, too little time leaves the middle cold, and the skin is almost always the first casualty. This is a practical comparison of the four methods people actually use for leftover turkey, with the times we use ourselves and the reason behind each one.

## Oven: the default that keeps the most moisture

Wrap the meat in foil with a splash of broth and set it on a wire rack over a rimmed baking sheet. At 325 degrees Fahrenheit, two servings of slices take about 22 to 28 minutes; a half breast needs 25 to 35 minutes. America's Test Kitchen's gentler version uses 275 degrees Fahrenheit and a foil pack on a rack, and their crosswise-cut half breast took 35 to 45 minutes. The lower temperature brings the center up slowly, so less water is pushed out of the meat.

## Microwave: fastest, least gentle

Two servings of slices take roughly 90 to 150 seconds on medium power, covered with a damp paper towel, turned or stirred halfway. A turkey burger needs 30 to 45 seconds. Microwaves heat the edges long before the middle, so stop early and let the plate stand for half a minute. If you want the skin back, finish the pieces in a dry skillet. The [turkey reheat calculator](https://tools.resuhive.com/how-long-to-reheat-turkey) shows the seconds version of the same estimate when you choose the microwave.

## Air fryer: good for small pieces

One layer at 350 degrees Fahrenheit: slices take three to five minutes, a turkey burger three to four, and larger pieces seven to ten. Shake the basket halfway. The fan is strong, so the gap between crisp and dry is short; check early. This is the best option for burgers and for anyone who wants a firmer surface without a skillet.

## Stovetop and slow cooker

A steamer basket over simmering water, covered, does slices in five to eight minutes and a breast half in twelve to eighteen. It keeps the meat moist without a microwave. A slow cooker on low with a quarter to a half cup of broth takes one to one and a half hours for slices and two and a half to three and a half hours for large pieces. It is nearly impossible to dry the meat that way, but it will not re-crisp skin.

## The rule that overrides all of them

The USDA says to refrigerate leftovers within two hours, eat them within three to four days, and reheat to 165 degrees Fahrenheit in the thickest part. Use the times above to decide when to start checking, not when to stop. A [food thermometer](https://tools.resuhive.com/how-long-to-reheat-turkey) is the only way to know, and the full guide on that page repeats the safety numbers alongside every method.
